Overview

I am a third-year PhD student in Behavioral Science at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business. Before that I studied public policy at the Harris School of Public Policy.

I study the role of uncertainty in judgment and decision making, across domains with explicit uncertainty (gambles, betting) and implicit uncertainty (multi-alternative, multi-attribute choice). Theoretically, I aim to understand the process by which uncertainty is integrated into decisions. Methodologically, I am interested in the elicitation of uncertainty and the application of different elicitation formats.

I apply these pursuits to choice anomalies, sports betting and prediction markets, and investment decisions, with an eye toward implications for policy and consumer protection.
